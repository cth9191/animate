# What goes in a frame

`STORY.md` and `FORMATS.md` cover structure: beats, pacing, cuts, sound. This file covers what a single frame holds.

It was written on 2026-10-03, after storyboard v1 of `pieces/history-of-ai` came back as "pretty boring … this isn't anything like those". That board drew each beat as a labelled diagram on blank paper. It was compared against Kevin's sheets: `dinosaurs`, `clawd-grandma`, `tortoise`, `what-do-you-love` (in `references/sheets/`, local only).

**Before drawing any storyboard or style frame, open 2–4 of Kevin's sheets in the target look, then check every panel against this list.**

## The checklist (every key frame)

1. **A character with a face, acting.** Almost every frame of his has a hero with eyes and a mood:
   - the hatchling dinosaur
   - Claude's asterisk with a face
   - Clawd, the tortoise, the people

   Things happen *to* the character, and it reacts (wow, sad, delighted, focused). A diagram is not a character.
2. **A full-bleed world.** The whole 9:16 is a place: wallpaper, sky, a table, gingham, a starry night, or a flat colour card that sets the beat's mood. Never an object floating on blank paper, unless blank is the point (dinosaurs' white page is a *stage*, with a ground line, a sun and plants).
3. **The medium shows.** Cut paper with torn edges and soft drop shadows; crayon or pencil texture in the fills; patterns (stripes, dots, gingham, plaid, quilting). Flat vector fills read as clip art.
4. **Small life in the background:** 3 or more secondary details per frame that don't carry the plot but make the world lived-in. For example:
   - a sleeping cat, birds on a wire, a moth at the lamp, steam from a mug
   - confetti, a clock, sticky notes, bunting

   He has dragonflies, beetles and ferns, a cat on the roof, paper planes.
5. **The idea is acted out literally.** "AI winter" is snow falling on a closed lab, with an empty piggy bank. "Everyone says hi" is a storm of chat bubbles and phones held up. Not a label.
6. **Text lives in the world.** Words on a note, a sign, a tag or a screen, hand-lettered. One short caption per beat at most.
7. **Scale varies across the board:** wides, mid shots, and at least one extreme close-up (the dinosaur's eye, the clock, a hand holding a phone). A board where every panel uses the same framing is a slideshow.
8. **A recurring thread across frames:** a callback prop or critter (the dinosaurs' yellow survivor, the tortoise's balloon, our cat and the framed 1950 page).

## Our hero device

For explainers with a long time span, give "the idea" a body: **the spark**, an orange starburst with a face. It gains a ray per era and ends as Claude's asterisk. Orange stays reserved for it.

## Process

1. Open the references.
2. Make **2–4 full-size style frames** for the look (the cheap gate).
3. Then the full storyboard in that look.
4. Then the video.

The style frames' drawing code becomes the video's code.
