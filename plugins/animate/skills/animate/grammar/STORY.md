# Story grammar: how Kevin's videos tell a story

Extracted by the story study (`pieces/study-story`, run 1, 2026-09-17) from shot logs of three story videos, with one control. Every rule below cites at least two of the three story videos; the control is noted when it breaks the rule.
- **Story videos** (`grammar/shotlogs/`): the fruit fly `[fly]`, the paper boat `[boat]` and `answer.html` `[answer]`.
- **Control:** the Fable 5.1 recreation `[control]`, music added afterwards.

**The one idea:** tell a *true process* as a story. The process gives the order, the hero carries you through it, and the pace changes with what each step means. Every cut has a reason you could say out loud.

Read the sections in order. **WHAT VARIES** is where our pieces adapt instead of copying.

**Scope (2026-10-02):** these rules come from three beat-cut videos. The formats study (16 videos) found that several are format-specific, not universal: the beat grid, the journey being fastest, travel as one camera move, the hero held in place, the handwritten title. Read `grammar/FORMATS.md` → "What STORY.md got wrong" before applying them to a piece that isn't format F5 (fixed-hero journey).

## THE TRUE PROCESS

- **The plot is a real process with its own order, so nothing needs explaining.** [fly 0–27s: egg → larva → pupa → adult → mates → eggs → the next egg] [boat 0–26s: written → folded → launched → voyage → arrives → unfolded] [answer 0–28s: a call → the world answers → every voice → alone → answered]. The control is a run of textures in a circle with no order [control 6–13s].
- **Each beat is one true step, shown by the image that step naturally produces.** [fly 7.21s the egg hatches onto the flesh it eats] [boat 1.50s the letter folds] [answer 4.5s the bell rings its own sound].
- **The process has a turn, a moment where it could go wrong or change direction.** [fly 16.3–19.2s the swatter] [boat 12.5–15s the great wave, the whale, lightning] [answer 17–18s alone again before the answer].
- **The subject is quietly about the maker, and the title says so.** [fly the next generation beside "opus 5"] [boat a letter from "opus 5 / claude"] [answer a voice answered, "opus 5 / claude"].

## THE HERO AND THE ANCHOR

- **One small, simple hero from first frame to last.** [fly the fly and its egg] [boat the paper boat, which is the letter] [answer the blue dot].
- **In the fastest section the hero holds its screen position while the world changes around it.** [fly 14.4–16.3s centred on the kitchen pan] [boat 8.5–17s fixed across ~20 worlds] [answer 12–15.5s the dot through 16th-note cuts].
- **The hero changes form but keeps one identity cue.** [fly spark → egg → larva → pupa → adult, always the red eyes once grown] [boat letter → boat → letter, the same paper and handwriting].

## THE PACE

- **Open still and quiet.** [fly 0–1.2s held, −21.8 dB] [boat 0–1.5s 100% held, −44 dB] [answer 0–1.5s −27 to −33 dB, one dot].
- **A build holds one framing while detail accrues, for 3–6s.** [fly 1.5–7.2s the egg divides and segments, 0 cuts] [boat 0–4s written and folded on one desk] [answer 0–4.5s ripple cycles around the dot]. The control also builds [control 0–3s], so a build alone doesn't make a story.
- **Travel is one continuous camera move, not cuts.** [fly 14.4–16.3s one pan stepped every 2 frames] [boat 4–8s the lake pan, 0% held frames].
- **The journey is the fastest section and speeds up on a fixed tempo.** [fly 7.2–9.3s a new image every ~0.3s, 12–16s motion 15–34] [boat 8.5–17s long-short-short, 3–6 cuts/s] [answer 6.5–15.5s 0.5s → 0.25s → 0.125s, 8 cuts/s]. The control cuts 5–8/s from the start, on no grid, with no acceleration [control 6–13s].
- **A turn builds slowly, then changes in 1–3 frames.** [fly 16.3–18.6s the shadow grows, then the slam at 18.58s] [answer 15.5–17s the mandala gathers every world, then drains to one dot] [boat 17–18s the moon swells, then the island].
- **The connection is held: 0–1 cuts/s for 3–7s.** [fly 19.2–22.2s courtship] [boat 18–21.5s arrival and unfolding, 0 cuts, 83–96% held] [answer 18–23s the orbit, 0 cuts].

## THE CUT

- **Every cut has a reason you could say out loud**, one of the link types in the table below. [fly 16 shots use all eight types] [boat next-step and match-position carry the voyage] [answer iris-out from the dot plus match-position]. The control's cuts are linked only by the eyepiece circle [control 6–13s].
- **Cuts land on the beat grid.** [fly 7.21–8.42s every 7 frames, the 8th at 100 BPM] [boat 120 BPM long-short-short] [answer 120 BPM, 16ths = 3 frames]. The control is on no grid.
- **Most "quick cuts" are steps of one move on 2s, not new shots.** [fly 58 diff spikes, 16 shots] [boat 57 spikes, 12 shots].
- **Late shots call back to early framings, changed.** [fly 11.5s the adult on the orange = 0s; 25.6s the spark = 1.3s] [boat 20s the desk = 0s] [answer 17s the dot alone = 0s; 14–15s the first worlds recoloured].

| link type | reason it gives | frame recipe | seen in |
|---|---|---|---|
| next-step | the process continues | hard cut on the grid, same world or the next one | fly 3→4, 5→6; boat 1→2→3 |
| match-position | the hero holds its spot, the world changes | hard cut or wipe; the hero is drawn identically across the cut | boat 6–8; answer 9–10; fly 9 |
| iris-in / iris-out | into or out of the hero | a circle on the hero: 2–4 frames (answer: r 410 → 650 → full) | fly 2; answer 2, 4, 7 |
| scale-in / scale-out | inside it, or everything around it | cut to the same subject 5–20× bigger or smaller | fly 5, 8; answer 11, 15 |
| pov | what the hero sees | cut to the hero's view, same moment | fly 7 |
| cause-effect | this made that happen | a build shot, then the effect on the grid (1–3 frames) | fly 10→11; answer 13 |
| time-cut | later | a flicker on 2s, or light/sky changes on the same framing | fly 12, 14, 15 |
| callback | back to an earlier image, changed | reuse an earlier framing exactly | fly 6, 16; boat 11; answer 12 |

## THE SOUND

- **Sound drops before a chapter turn, 8–30 dB for about a second, while the picture holds.** [fly −29.8 dB at 1s (dive), −25.8 at 10s (emergence), −21.6 at 17s (before the swatter), −29.2 at 24s (dew)] [answer −43.9 dB at 17s, before the answer] [boat −24.5 at 3s after the fold, −19.5 at 19s at the island]. The control dips inside a cutting run instead [control −28.9 at 10s, −30 at 12s].
- **The loudest single hit marks a turn, not the busiest cutting.** [fly −8.7 dB at 18.5s, the slam] [boat −11.0 at 8.0s, the launch] [answer −12.4 at 16.1s, the end of the peak, one second before silence].
- **The journey is a flat groove: loudness steady within ~3 dB for 5–9s.** [boat 8.5–17s −14.5 to −18.4] [fly 12–16s −11.1 to −12.3] [answer 6–11s −23.8 to −26.7].
- **Each step or world voices itself.** [answer an owl hoots, a bell rings, a foghorn sounds] [boat a bright pencil scratch while the letter is written].

## THE TITLE AND THE LOOP

- **A handwritten title is written on in the last 2–4s, next to the hero.** [fly 25.6–27s "opus 5" beside the new egg] [boat 21.5–26s "opus 5 / claude" over the night sea] [answer 26–28s beside the two dots]. The control does this too [control 17–23s], so it's a signature, not what makes the story work.
- **The ending returns to the opening image, changed by the story.** [fly a new egg, the next generation] [boat the letter on the desk, now read] [answer one dot becomes two].

## WHAT VARIES

- **Beat order.**
  - fly: start → build → change → journey → turn → connection → time passes → quiet → title
  - answer: start → journey → peak → silence → connection → title
  - boat: build → travel → journey → quiet → connection → title
- **Where the loudest sustained section sits:** the connection in `answer` (24–25s), the growth and journey plateaus in `fly` (−11 dB), the voyage in `boat`.
- **Number of shots:** 12 (boat), 16 (fly), 16 (answer).
- **How fixed the hero is:** fixed on screen the whole way (answer, boat) or only while travelling (fly).
- **Tempo and medium:** 100 or 120 BPM; crosshatch, flat picture book or riso.
- **Time devices:** a day/night flicker with tally marks appears only in the fly.
- **The ending:** a literal loop (fly, answer) or a fade (boat).
- **What the turn is:** danger (fly), the scale of the world (boat), loneliness (answer).

## Our notes that were wrong

1. **"His arc is start → change → journey → peak → silence → connection."** Only `answer` runs in that order. The fly puts the connection before the quiet beat, and the boat has no separate peak. The order varies; the beat *types* and their pace are what recur.
2. **"The connection is the loudest moment."** That's true only for `answer`'s sustained level. The fly's loudest hit is the swatter slam (−8.7 dB at 18.5s), and the boat's is the launch (−11.0 dB at 8.0s). The rule that holds is that the loudest hit marks a turn.
3. **"The fruit fly has a silence act."** It has four one-second dips, one before each chapter, not a single silence act.
4. **"`answer` is loudest at 24s, −2.3 dB."** By 100ms RMS, the loudest single hit is at 16.1s (−12.4 dB) and the loudest sustained section is 24–25s (median −13.6). The −2.3 figure was a different, peak-level measure.
5. **"The fruit fly is fast-cut."** It has 16 shots in 27s. The speed comes from steps of one move on 2s (58 diff spikes), not from many shots.
